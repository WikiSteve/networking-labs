## Summary

This reusable lecture deck introduces Internet of Things concepts from a networking and systems perspective, with strong emphasis on how connected devices generate, move, and depend on data across real infrastructure. It defines IoT as physical objects with sensors, software, and connectivity, then walks through the core components of IoT systems: sensors, actuators, embedded systems, microcontrollers versus single-board computers, connectivity protocols, cloud services, and a three-layer IoT architecture spanning devices, networks or gateways, and cloud applications. It uses practical examples such as smart thermostats, connected cars, smart manufacturing, healthcare, smart homes, and smart cities to show how Wi-Fi, BLE, Zigbee, Z-Wave, LTE-M, and NB-IoT fit different range, bandwidth, and power constraints.

## Key Details

- Defines IoT as a network of physical objects with sensors, software, and connectivity, and stresses that the network is as important as the devices.
- Covers hardware building blocks such as sensors, actuators, and embedded systems.
- Distinguishes microcontrollers like Arduino from single-board computers like Raspberry Pi.
- Introduces connectivity options and tradeoffs: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or BLE, Zigbee, Z-Wave, LTE-M, and NB-IoT.
- Explains the cloud's role in storage, processing, remote control, automation, device management, and analytics.
- Organizes IoT into a three-layer model: devices, networks or gateways, and cloud or application services.
- Uses practical examples such as smart thermostats, connected cars, and smart manufacturing to show end-to-end data flow.
- Includes discussion prompts aimed at infrastructure thinking, such as scale, robotics support, real-time handling, and backend design.
- Highlights network-management concerns such as monitoring many devices, automating updates, and using segmentation to reduce operational pain.
- Covers security risks including weak defaults, poor authentication, lack of TLS, patching problems, and enlarged attack surfaces.
- Recommends segmentation with VLANs, stronger device management, regular patching, and a zero-trust mindset.
- Ends with a Wireshark-based exercise on identifying visible device traffic and whether it is encrypted.
